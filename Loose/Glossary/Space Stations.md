# Space Stations

Space stations are the ports, depots and habitats of the [[Immediate Stellar Cluster]]. Nearly all of them sit far from the worlds they serve.

## Siting

What matters in choosing a site is how fast a station moves relative to its world. Every vessel that arrives, and every craft that climbs up from the surface, must match the station's velocity before it can dock. The slower a station moves, the cheaper it is to reach. Distance matters far less, because an isolation drive crosses hundreds of thousands of kilometres in seconds.

For an Earth-like world:

| Site | Station's speed relative to the world | Velocity change from the surface |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low orbit (~400 km) | ~7.7 km/s | ~7–8 km/s |
| Synchronous orbit | ~3.1 km/s | ~2.6–3.5 km/s |
| High orbit (~400,000 km) | ~1.0 km/s | ~0.5–1.5 km/s |
| Star–planet L1 or L2 (~1.5 million km) | ~0.3 km/s | up to ~0.8 km/s |

Stations therefore occupy high orbits and Lagrange points: the star–planet L1 and L2 points, and the L4 and L5 points of a world and a sufficiently massive moon. There they need only very minor corrections to stay on station, and vessels parked nearby barely drift relative to one another. Low orbits are left to specialised traffic. Synchronous orbits, which hold a station over a fixed point on the ground, are reserved for future surface-to-orbit infrastructure.

## Port Operations

Port traffic control assigns every arriving vessel a parking slot. Slots are deconflicted orbits, Lagrange-point halos or powered holds, so no two parked vessels share a path. Approach corridors lead to each station, and docking is automated.

Unattended vessels keep watch for themselves. Their navigation computers screen for close approaches whenever traffic, debris or the station's surroundings change, and make small avoidance burns without waiting for their crews.
